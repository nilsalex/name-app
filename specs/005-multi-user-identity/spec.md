# Feature Specification: Multi-User Identity

**Feature Branch**: `005-multi-user-identity`

**Created**: 2026-11-02

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "data should be stored under unique identifiers
now, NOT from mapping email addresses to person a/b — for scaling to more
users, potentially"

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-11-02

- Q: Where do the two approved Gmail addresses live — in committed config or
  as secret environment variables? → A: Secret environment variables — the
  addresses are stored like other credentials (never committed); the alias
  mapping address→A/B is part of that same secret configuration.
- Q: What replaces the fixed A/B alias as the per-user identifier in vote
  records? → A: Stable internal user IDs — generated by the app, opaque
  (non-enumerable), persisted server-side; the email is verified at login
  but never stored in vote records.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Log in with my Gmail address (Priority: P1)

A user opens the app. Instead of picking "Person A" or "Person B" from a
list, they see a login option that lets them sign in with their Gmail
address (Google account). After signing in successfully, they land directly
in the vote screen already identified as their own persistent user — no
extra identity selection step. The sign-in persists across visits: returning
to the app later, they are still logged in and go straight to voting.

**Why this priority**: This is the entire feature — without working login
there is no identity concept to build on. It replaces the manual identity
selection as the app's entry point.

**Independent Test**: Open the app in a fresh browser session, sign in with
an approved Gmail address, and verify the vote screen appears directly,
attributed to the correct user; close and reopen the app and verify the
session is still active.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an unauthenticated visitor, **When** they open the app, **Then**
   they see a login screen with a "Sign in with Google" option (no Person
   A/B selection buttons anywhere).
2. **Given** the login screen, **When** the user signs in with an approved
   Gmail address, **Then** they land on the vote screen and the app shows
   their stable user identity without further prompts.
3. **Given** a signed-in user, **When** they reload or revisit the app,
   **Then** they remain signed in and go straight to voting (no repeated
   login for the same browser session lifecycle until sign-out or expiry).

---

### User Story 2 - Votes are attributed to the real logged-in account (Priority: P2)

When a logged-in user votes, the vote is recorded against their real,
verified account via a stable internal user ID — not a positional alias
derived from their address. Votes can no longer be attributed to the wrong
person, and the identification scheme scales to any number of users without
schema changes.

**Why this priority**: Correct attribution is the point of replacing manual
selection with login; it is what makes the login more than a cosmetic
change. It builds directly on US1's session.

**Independent Test**: Sign in as a user, cast a vote, and verify the vote is
recorded under that user's stable internal ID (not "A"/"B", not an email);
repeat with a second user and verify two distinct IDs.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a signed-in user, **When** they cast a vote, **Then** the
   recorded vote carries their stable internal user ID.
2. **Given** two different users have voted previously, **When** any
   per-user view is opened, **Then** each user's activity is distinguishable
   by their distinct internal IDs.
3. **Given** a user signs in on a different device, **When** they vote,
   **Then** the same internal user ID is used (derived from the account, not
   the browser).

---

### User Story 3 - Sign out and access control (Priority: P3)

A signed-in user can actively sign out, which ends their session and returns
them to the login screen. Only approved accounts may proceed past the login
screen; anyone else who tries is shown a clear "no access" message and
cannot vote. Approval is per-account and manageable without redeploying.

**Why this priority**: Trust and hygiene — the ability to consciously end a
session and the guarantee that strangers cannot vote. It is a safety net
around US1 rather than a daily-use capability.

**Independent Test**: Sign in, sign out, verify the login screen returns and
the vote screen is unreachable without re-login; attempt access with a
non-approved Gmail address and verify it is refused with a friendly message.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a signed-in user, **When** they choose "Sign out", **Then**
   their session ends and they see the login screen again.
2. **Given** a signed-out user, **When** they attempt to reach the vote
   screen (e.g., via a bookmark), **Then** they are sent back to the login
   screen and cannot vote.
3. **Given** a Google account whose address is not approved, **When** they
   complete the Google sign-in flow, **Then** they see a clear, friendly
   "no access" message and no vote UI is shown.

### Edge Cases

- What happens when the Google sign-in flow is interrupted (user closes the
  popup / cancels consent)? → The app returns to the login screen with no
  partial state; no vote possibility exists.
- What happens when a session expires while mid-voting? → The next action
  leads back to a re-login step; the in-flight UI resets cleanly, no vote is
  recorded under a stale identity.
- What happens to historical votes recorded under aliases A/B before login
  existed? → They keep their original attribution values unchanged; the
  ranking and winners pages must continue to render with the old data
  alongside votes recorded under new-style user IDs.
- What happens when the sign-in service is briefly unavailable? → The login
  screen explains the failure and offers a retry; the app never reveals vote
  content while unauthenticated.
- What happens when the same person signs in with a different Google
  account? → They are treated as a different user with their own identity;
  no automatic merging occurs.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: The app MUST replace the manual "Person A / Person B"
  selection screen with a sign-in flow as the only way to obtain a vote
  identity.
- **FR-002**: Users MUST be able to sign in using their Google (Gmail)
  account via the standard Google sign-in experience.
- **FR-003**: The system MUST grant access only to approved Gmail addresses
  and MUST show a friendly denial message to any other account.
- **FR-004**: Every account that signs in successfully MUST be represented
  by a stable, unique internal user ID that is: app-generated (never derived
  from the email address itself), opaque (not enumerable or guessable from
  other users' data), and persistent (the same account resolves to the same
  ID on every future sign-in).
- **FR-005**: Vote records MUST be attributed to the stable internal user ID
  of the logged-in account at vote time.
- **FR-006**: The signed-in state MUST persist across reloads and revisits
  within the same browser until sign-out or expiry.
- **FR-007**: The signed-in state MUST NOT outlive a deliberate sign-out;
  after sign-out, no vote UI is reachable without re-login and the open
  session is terminated on the spot.
- **FR-008**: The user's email address or account details MUST NOT be stored
  in vote records or shown anywhere in the app; only neutral labels (e.g.,
  "Person 1") are displayed, if any.
- **FR-009**: Unauthenticated access to any voting capability MUST be
  impossible (login gate in front of the vote screen).
- **FR-010**: If the login flow fails or is cancelled, the app MUST show the
  login screen again with a short, honest error hint and lose no state.
- **FR-011**: The approved-address list MUST remain secret configuration
  (environment variables), never committed, and MUST be extendable to more
  addresses without code changes or data migration.
- **FR-012**: The identifier scheme MUST NOT embed personal information
  (email, name) in any stored or displayed identifier.

### Key Entities *(include if data model involved)*

- **Account**: A person's real identity behind the login — a Gmail address
  plus the stable internal user ID the app resolves it to. The number of
  accounts is bounded only by the approved list.
- **User**: A stored identity record: stable internal ID plus whatever
  metadata the app needs; created on first approved sign-in, reused
  thereafter.
- **Session**: The signed-in state in a browser — exists from successful
  login until sign-out or expiry; determines whether vote UI is reachable.
- **Internal user ID**: App-generated opaque identifier that stands in for
  the account everywhere data is stored; never contains the email.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: A returning approved user goes from opening the app to seeing
  their vote screen in under 5 seconds with zero identity-selection steps.
- **SC-002**: A first-time approved user completes sign-in within 60
  seconds.
- **SC-003**: 100% of votes after launch are attributed to the internal user
  ID of the actually signed-in account (no vote can be cast under the wrong
  identity, and no stored identifier contains an email address).
- **SC-004**: 100% of non-approved accounts that complete the Google flow
  are stopped at the login screen with the denial message.
- **SC-005**: Sign-out returns the user to the login screen and leaves no
  reachable vote UI in that browser afterwards.
- **SC-006**: Adding one more approved address requires editing exactly one
  piece of configuration and no code or data migration.

## Assumptions

- The approved addresses remain deployment secrets (environment variables
  in `.env.local` locally and the hosting platform's secret store in
  production); they are never committed.
- Google sign-in is used via Google's hosted login experience; the app does
  not build its own email/password system and does not send email.
- No personal data beyond the opaque internal user ID and minimal
  session/request metadata is stored; vote records keep their existing shape
  except that the identity field holds the internal user ID.
- Historical votes (pre-004 aliases "A"/"B") remain untouched; the ranking
  and winners pages must tolerate mixed legacy and new-style identifiers.
- Access control happens after Google's authentication so the approved-list
  check is based on a verified address, not a typed one.
- A missing or misconfigured address secret is an operator error to fix at
  deployment time, not a user-facing state; the app fails fast at startup
  rather than serving a broken login.
- Scaling beyond two users is a configuration change only; the app imposes
  no fixed user count.
